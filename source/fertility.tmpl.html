Director’s Statement

A horror mystery born of dread and fate.

A couple who cannot have a child are offered a surrogate. She is young, beautiful, kind, and she carries an ancient bloodline that is coming for the baby. Everything that should be safe, the best clinic, a contract, a healthy surrogate. What’s revealed is what was there the whole time, waiting for the seed to take.

A simple want, for a family of their own pushes everyone forward. That’s how the bloodline works: a woman offers herself, and by offering herself she takes everything. The surrogate carrying Roman’s child is the woman who once carried Roman. Together they will be born again. It’s impossible. And yet. There’s fate in blood.

At the centre is a man who built his whole life on the belief that he chose his path. FERTILITY is the story of Roman learning that his future was chosen before he was born. That legacy and family are already his and it requires a terrible choice. His wife Shauna refuses to see what she already knows, that what she wants more than anything has already escaped her. They wanted a child and their true desires are revealed.

This is a film in the lineage of Rosemary’s Baby, Dead Ringers, and Hereditary. When the house burns, the bloodline lives on.

The Clinic

The Fertility Clinic

This is where Roman and Shauna go to buy a future: the most legitimate room in the film, elite and immaculate and reassuring. It is also where the bloodline performs its rite. The surrogacy is real medicine and an ancient ceremony at once, and the film never lets you pull them apart. The silver instrument is for insemination and sacrament. Behind the one-way glass, conception is watched like a service. Nothing in the room says horror. All of it is.

The Look · Palette & the Uncanny

Dread has a Cool Palette. Warmth is a Bloody Event.

Blue-hour atmosphere, slate, silver-green, diffused grey. Warmth never washes a room. It exists only as a source you can point at. A lamp in the window. A flesh tone. A fire.

The uncanny is one displaced element

Every frame is built to read correct, a country house on a perfect evening, with a single element precisely inexplicably wrong. This is the governing mechanic.

Captured on 35mm film, with vintage glass, modern lighting, in-camera effects.

The Uncanny Familiar

The Familiar Turned Quietly Wrong

The German word for the uncanny is unheimlich: literally unhomely, the home made strange. It is the engine of the film. A sense it’s not right. A photograph a character cannot unsee.

FERTILITY turns on a Polaroid, dated decades before it should exist, an image that proves the present was always a repetition. The horror is never announced. It is recognised, slowly, as something the audience already knew.

The Lead

A Principled Man

Roman is the film’s moral centre: a principled man who meets evil couched in the most genuine desire to do right, by his wife, for the family they have not been able to make. Every loving choice tightens the trap.

He is decent in the register of James Stewart: sure of his own goodness, earnest in his intentions, in his desire for a family. That is exactly what the bloodline requires. The role asks its lead to be sincere in his intentions, so that the audience goes all the way with him.

His Wife

Refuses to See What She Knows

Shauna wants two things: a baby, and her husband. She refuses to see what she already knows. Her body performs a phantom pregnancy. She reduces the unimaginable to something survivable.

Then the baby will not feed from her. Motherhood collapses at its most primal. Her instinct finally surfaces: ‘I knew it.’ She demands the truth in a lawyer’s language, and leaves. If she returns, it’s to face the fire and the truth.

The Surrogate

Youth Beauty Fertile

Leah is the offering: young, luminous, barefoot, chosen for a body that works. The audience can see the appeal and yet sense that something is amiss. She hums to the baby through her own skin. That is the trap, and it is laid with beauty and warmth.

The Women

Ascension by Submission

The women of FERTILITY hold power precisely by seeming to give themselves away. Chani, the surrogate, the mother: each rules through display, through tenderness, through the pose of service.

The men believe they are choosing. The women have already arranged the room. It is the pin-up’s oldest trick, taken seriously as a theology: the body offered is the body in command.

The Set-Pieces

Iconic Scenes

01 · The insemination

The procedure plays as a miracle and a rite at once. Hope and dread in one frame, and both are telling the truth.

02 · The womb, from the inside

At the midpoint the camera goes inside the surrogate: the child present at its own making. Not a dream or diversion. The truth is gestating as well.

03 · The rite’s own rules, turned into a weapon

The ceremony has one requirement it cannot proceed without. Roman is the first to understand that its rules can be used against it. He does not fight the rite. He holds it hostage.

04 · The mis-strike

The coven never overpowers Roman. It seduces him. A vision of milk and mother rises at the instant the blade is raised, a small gesture planted an hour earlier fires under the spell, and the knife comes down on the wrong body. Theme turned into muscle memory.

05 · The revelation

A rational man runs out of rational explanations. What’s impossible is true.

06 · The molt

The house burns and the bloodline does not. In the ash an old woman’s shell splits, a new surrogate steps out into the morning.

Four films. A genre all their own.
